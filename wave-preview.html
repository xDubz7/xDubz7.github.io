Waveform styles — pick one

All four are playing the same fake DJ mix (intro → build → drop → breakdown → drop) so you can compare fairly. These are mock-ups with made-up data — the real ones are computed from each song file, the same way rekordbox analyzes tracks. Tell me the letter you want.

A

Rekordbox 3-Band

Each bar is stacked from 3 frequency bands: blue = bass/kick at the base, orange = mids (synths, vocals) in the middle, white = highs (hats, snares) at the tips. You can literally see a drop coming: the blue gets fat.

Played part is bright, what's ahead is dimmed. White line = playhead.

B

Rekordbox RGB

Same three bands, but blended into one color per bar instead of stacked: red = bass, green = mids, blue = highs. Where everything hits at once it blends toward white — drops glow pale, bass-only parts burn red.

The other rekordbox mode — most information, busier look.

C

MiniMeters scrolling

No full-song overview — a clean filled waveform that scrolls: the last few seconds of the song flow past, like a live meter. Minimal, one color, very “producer tool.” You lose the ability to see the whole song / tap to seek on it (seeking would stay on the mini progress bar).

Playhead is fixed at the right edge; the song flows underneath it.

D

Theme waveform + live spectrum below

Keep the current per-theme waveform on top (this mock uses Shadow's red → gold), and add a spectrum analyzer strip underneath that dances with whatever is playing at that moment — bass on the left, highs on the right, MiniMeters Spectrum-style. The spectrum's colors replace nothing; the themes stay in charge up top.

Top: theme waveform. Bottom: spectrum of the exact moment under the playhead.
